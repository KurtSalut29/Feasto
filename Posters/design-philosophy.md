# Warm Radius

**A design philosophy for a town that gets fed from its own streets.**

The ground is real. Behind every composition lies the actual town: its coastline, the grid of its old center, the long roads running out to the barangays. They are drawn as plain lines in the page's own color, quiet enough to read as texture, true enough that anyone who lives there recognizes home. Nothing is invented to look technical; the map is the only "data" on the page, and it is the right data.

At the center stands a warm disc, like a lamp over a dinner table, and the figure stands inside it. The figure faces the work: the chef's open hand and the rider's forward motion both point to the phone beside them, so the eye travels from person to product without being told to. The phone shows the real app, never a mock-up, slightly turned toward the figure, as if it is being handed over.

Color is appetite, rationed. One color owns each piece: rice-paper cream, ember orange, or the blue-black of a street after dark. A second tone answers it, and one bright accent is spent only where the eye must land. The three pieces share a temperature, not a template, so they read as one family in a row.

Type is plain and confident. A friendly heavy grotesque is set large and tight, two short lines of everyday Taglish, the way a neighbor would say it. One quiet sentence explains. Small labels are ordinary words in ordinary type, never spaced-out capitals or coded markers. If a line would not be said out loud in town, it does not go on the page.

Restraint is the craft. Wide, equal margins; nothing touching the edge; nothing overlapping that should not. When something feels empty, enlarge what is already there instead of adding a new ornament. Each piece should look labored over by hand, pixel by pixel, until it stops asking to be fixed, and never like it came out of a machine.
